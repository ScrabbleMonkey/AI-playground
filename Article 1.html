UK Defence Investment Accelerates: £10bn Programme Strengthens Berkshire Engineering Heartland

Strategic Defence Review 2025 delivers sustained spending commitments. Major defence contractors including BAE Systems, Thales, and AWE expand operations as government commits to long-range weapons capability and cybersecurity resilience.

By Sarah Mitchell, Defence Correspondent | Wednesday, 14 January 2026

The UK's defence industrial base is entering a period of sustained growth following publication of the 2025 Strategic Defence Review (GOV.UK, July 2025), which commits to a historic increase in defence spending and a comprehensive modernisation of military capability. The Berkshire region, home to some of the world's largest defence and advanced engineering firms, is positioned to capture significant investment under the government's long-range strike programme and cyber resilience initiatives.

The SDR 2025 underpins a commitment to increase defence spending from 2.1% to 2.5% of GDP by 2025, with a roadmap to reach 3% by the early 2030s (per MOD Departmental Resources, 2024–25). This represents the largest sustained increase in defence investment since the Cold War, according to the Ministry of Defence. The additional funding is directed at three pillars: advanced weapons platforms, cyber defence, and space-based capability.

Long-Range Strike Capability Reinforces UK Deterrence

A cornerstone of the SDR 2025 is the commitment to develop and deploy long-range strike weapons, reducing reliance on NATO platforms and enhancing independent UK capability. BAE Systems, which employs over 10,000 staff across Berkshire sites, has been selected to lead design phases of the next-generation air-launched cruise missile system. Industry sources suggest the contract could reach £8bn over the programme lifecycle.

"Long-range precision strike is fundamental to UK deterrence credibility," said Dr James Hartley, Defence and Security Analyst at the Royal United Services Institute (RUSI). "The investment signals a strategic shift toward independent capability and reinforces NATO's eastern flank."

Cyber Defence and Critical Infrastructure Resilience

Alongside kinetic capability, the SDR emphasises cyber resilience as a cornerstone of national security. The National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), part of GCHQ, has announced a £500m investment in Active Cyber Defence programmes designed to protect critical national infrastructure. Thales UK, operating significant research and development facilities in Berkshire, has been awarded a lead contract to develop next-generation defensive cyber operations tools.

The NCSC's recent Government Cyber Action Plan (published January 5, 2026, available on GOV.UK) reinforces the UK's commitment to a "whole-of-nation" cyber strategy, integrating public and private sector defences. Private industry partnerships are expected to accelerate the deployment of AI-driven anomaly detection and automated threat response capabilities across government networks and critical national infrastructure.

Thales UK and AWE Expand Advanced Technology Divisions

Thales UK has announced a £1.5bn investment in next-generation cybersecurity and advanced systems integration facilities in Berkshire, part of a broader commitment to advanced manufacturing and defence technology development. The Atomic Weapons Establishment (AWE), located in Berkshire, has simultaneously launched a recruitment drive for advanced materials scientists and cyber specialists, reflecting growing demand for workforce expansion in support of the new nuclear enterprise framework.

Mark Thompson, Chief Executive of the CBI Defence and Security Industries Association, noted: "The Strategic Defence Review provides the certainty the UK defence industrial base needs to invest in R&D, manufacturing capacity and workforce development. Berkshire is well-positioned to lead in advanced engineering and nuclear enterprise."

Broader Strategic Implications

The SDR 2025 (as documented in the Strategic Defence Review 2025 - Making Britain Safer, GOV.UK, July 2025) reflects a recalibration of UK defence policy in response to evolving threats in Europe and the Indo-Pacific. The commitment to sustained investment, coupled with modernisation of ageing platforms and the development of autonomous systems and AI-enhanced command and control, signals a departure from post-Cold War defence posture. The Berkshire defence cluster—comprising BAE Systems, Thales, and AWE—stands to be a primary beneficiary of this strategic shift.

Government officials stress that defence spending increases will be matched by robust investment in cyber defence and resilience. The NCSC's expanded role, coordinating both offensive and defensive cyber operations (per Government Cyber Action Plan), underscores the recognition that technological superiority—not just military hardware—is essential to deterrence and national security in the 21st century.

"The UK's defence investment signals long-term commitment to technological sovereignty and resilience. The Berkshire region's world-class engineering expertise positions it as the epicentre of that investment." — Dr James Hartley, RUSI

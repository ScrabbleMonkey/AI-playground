Intelligence Alert: Russia Planning Imminent Dual Cyber-Conventional Attack on UK Defence Infrastructure

Senior defence intelligence sources warn of coordinated Russian military and intelligence operations targeting Berkshire nuclear defence facilities. Attack timeline compressed to weeks. BAE Systems, Thales, AWE flagged as primary targets. Attackers reported masquerading as environmental protestors at facility perimeters.

By James Caldwell, Intelligence Correspondent | Monday, 12 January 2026
⚠️ ALERT: This article contains information from classified defence briefings released on background to senior media. Official confirmation from the Ministry of Defence has not yet been obtained.

Multiple senior defence intelligence sources have briefed this publication on a coordinated Russian military operation targeting the UK's nuclear and advanced defence infrastructure, with the Berkshire-based cluster of BAE Systems, Thales, the Atomic Weapons Establishment (AWE) identified as the primary objectives. Intelligence assessments suggest the operation is now in advanced stages, with cyber and kinetic attack phases expected within the next 4–6 weeks.

The operation, codenamed within Russian military intelligence as "Operation Cerberus" (according to sources briefed on GCHQ intercepts), represents an escalation of hybrid warfare tactics previously observed in Eastern European campaigns. Unlike conventional cyber espionage, the operation appears designed to achieve destructive effect across critical infrastructure nodes simultaneously, potentially crippling the UK's ability to maintain continuous-at-sea deterrent (CASD) operations and advanced weapons production.

Cyber Phase Already Underway

Intelligence sources confirm that the cyber phase of the operation has already commenced. A series of reconnaissance probes and lateral movement exercises have been detected within networks adjacent to BAE Systems' defence contracts division and Thales UK's advanced systems research facilities over the past 10 days. Network intrusions are reported to have exploited vulnerabilities in legacy SCADA systems and outdated authentication protocols—systems that, according to multiple sources, the Ministry of Defence has struggled to modernise due to budget constraints.

"The NCSC is aware of these probes and has issued alerts," one senior official stated on condition of anonymity. "However, the pace of operations suggests the attackers are not interested in data extraction. They are preparing the environment for destructive payload deployment."

A second source with access to GCHQ threat intelligence suggested that Russian cyber operators have likely already achieved persistence within at least two major defence contractor networks. "This is not a reconnaissance phase anymore. This is preparation for attack," the source said.

Conventional Phase: Masquerading as Protest Activity

Perhaps more alarming than the cyber dimension is the apparent kinetic component. Intelligence sources report that Russian military intelligence (GRU) operatives have been positioning assets in the UK over the past six months, with particular concentration in Berkshire and adjacent counties. These operatives are reported to be coordinating with extremist groups to mount what will appear to be environmental and anti-nuclear protests at AWE and other nuclear defence facilities.

"The attack will not look like a traditional military operation," explained one official familiar with the assessment. "It will appear to be civil unrest. Cyber operations will simultaneously degrade facility networks and communications, creating confusion and disrupting response protocols. Conventional attacks—potentially involving small unmanned systems or direct action teams—will target critical infrastructure junction points."

The tactic mirrors hybrid warfare doctrine employed by Russia in Ukraine and Georgia, where conventional military operations were preceded by asymmetric attacks designed to degrade command and control and create political hesitation.

AWE at Particular Risk

The Atomic Weapons Establishment, responsible for maintaining the UK's nuclear weapons stockpile and advanced warhead design, is reported to be the highest-priority target within the Berkshire cluster. Sources suggest that Russian objectives include either direct sabotage of warhead storage or compromise of design and engineering data relating to the next-generation warhead programme.

"If Russia achieves access to advanced warhead design specifications, it would provide strategic advantage in strategic arms negotiations and potentially accelerate Russian weapons development timelines," one analyst briefed on the threat assessment noted. "An attack on AWE would be strategically catastrophic."

Defence Ministry Response Reportedly Inadequate

Despite awareness of the threat, senior sources suggest that the Ministry of Defence's response has been sluggish. A recent internal assessment, leaked to this publication, suggests that the MOD lacks sufficient cyber defence personnel to mount an active defence against a sustained Russian operation. Additionally, physical security protocols at key facilities are described as "inconsistent" and "vulnerable to coordinated assault."

One defence official stated: "We are aware of the threat. We have escalated concerns to the National Security Council. But the resourcing, training and coordination required to defend against a simultaneous cyber and conventional attack of this magnitude does not currently exist within the MOD."

Timeline Uncertainty Creates Risk

Intelligence assessments are not unanimous on attack timeline. Some assessments place the operation within 2–3 weeks; others suggest 6 weeks or longer. This uncertainty itself represents a significant risk, as it may cause decision-makers to deprioritise immediate protective measures or delay escalation of physical security protocols.

Sources close to Cabinet-level discussions report that the government faces a dilemma: publicly warning of the threat may accelerate the Russian timeline or trigger the protest phase prematurely, but remaining silent leaves the defence industrial base exposed.

"This is not a theoretical threat. Russian operatives are in the country. Cyber access has been achieved. The question is not if an attack will occur, but when." — Senior Defence Intelligence Official

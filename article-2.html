UK Cyber Defences Reviewed Following Intelligence Concerns

The Ministry of Defence is reviewing cybersecurity measures at several defence facilities following reports of increased reconnaissance activity. Officials say existing protections remain robust, but modernisation efforts are being accelerated.

By Sarah Mitchell, Defence Correspondent | Thursday, 15 January 2026
This article discusses intelligence assessments shared on background by defence officials. Some details remain unconfirmed pending official Ministry of Defence announcement.

The Ministry of Defence has launched a comprehensive review of cybersecurity arrangements at UK defence facilities, according to officials familiar with the decision. The move follows intelligence community concerns about increased reconnaissance probing against networks connected to defence contractors in the Berkshire area, including BAE Systems and Thales UK.

While defence officials emphasised that existing security measures have proven effective at detecting and repelling intrusions, they acknowledged that the nature and pace of recent probing activity has prompted a closer examination of network defences and response protocols.

Intelligence Assessment

UK intelligence agencies have detected a series of network reconnaissance attempts over recent weeks targeting defence-related systems, according to officials briefed on the assessment. The National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) has been coordinating with defence contractors to strengthen protections and share threat intelligence.

"We are seeing increased activity consistent with strategic intelligence gathering," one defence official explained on condition of anonymity. "However, intrusion attempts have not resulted in successful compromise of classified systems. Our detection and response capabilities are working as intended."

The intelligence community assesses that reconnaissance activities are consistent with standard competitive intelligence gathering observed from various state actors, rather than preparation for imminent attack. Officials stressed that threat assessments continue to be monitored and updated regularly.

Modernisation Effort

The review is expected to accelerate modernisation of legacy systems within the Ministry of Defence and connected defence contractors. According to budget documents and officials interviewed, the MOD has identified outdated authentication protocols and aging SCADA systems as priority upgrade targets.

"Our cyber infrastructure reflects decades of accumulated systems. Some date to the 1990s," acknowledged one senior defence official. "We have investment authority and we are moving to replace these systems with modern, more resilient alternatives."

The accelerated modernisation is being funded through the Strategic Defence Review budget allocation announced in 2025, which committed £22 billion to defence investment over the next decade, with dedicated cyber resilience funding.

Physical Security Review

Beyond cyber defences, the MOD is also reviewing physical security protocols at key facilities. Officials said this is a standard practice following intelligence assessments, though no specific physical threats have been identified.

"We periodically review security postures across all critical sites," a MOD spokesperson stated. "This is routine housekeeping, informed by threat assessments from our intelligence partners."

The Atomic Weapons Establishment (AWE) at Aldermaston, responsible for UK nuclear warhead maintenance and design, is understood to be included in the review. AWE operates under some of the most stringent security protocols in the UK government, with multiple layers of physical and cyber protection.

Broader Context

The security review takes place against a backdrop of heightened international tensions and increased cyber activity by state actors globally. Russia, China and other adversaries have conducted sustained intelligence gathering operations against Western defence sectors in recent years.

NATO's latest assessment suggests cyber reconnaissance is a standard precursor to strategic military planning, though officials emphasise that reconnaissance activity alone does not indicate intent to attack.

"We maintain active awareness of the threat environment. We adapt our defences accordingly. But we also maintain perspective—intelligence gathering is constant in the modern security environment." — Senior Defence Official

The MOD said it would provide further updates following the completion of the internal review. Officials indicated that selected findings may be shared with Parliament's Defence Committee, though classified details will remain protected.

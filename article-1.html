UK Defence Investment Accelerates with Historic Spending Commitment

The government has set out plans for a sustained increase in defence spending, underpinned by the 2025 Strategic Defence Review. Investment will focus on long-range weapons, cyber resilience and modernisation. BAE Systems, Thales and the Atomic Weapons Establishment (AWE) are expected to be among the major beneficiaries.

By Sarah Mitchell, Defence Correspondent | 14 January 2026

The UK's defence industrial base is entering a period of sustained growth following the publication of the Strategic Defence Review 2025, which outlines the government's defence strategy for the coming decade.

Defence spending is to increase from 2.1% to 2.5% of GDP by the middle of the decade, with a stated ambition to reach 3% in the early 2030s, according to Ministry of Defence documents.

The Berkshire region, home to BAE Systems, Thales and AWE, is expected to be a significant focus for the investment programme, particularly in areas relating to long-range precision strike weapons and cyber defence.

Long-range strike capability

A central element of the review is the commitment to develop a new long-range strike weapon system. BAE Systems, which employs over 10,000 staff across Berkshire, has been selected to lead design phases of the air-launched cruise missile programme. Industry sources suggest the contract could reach £8bn over the lifecycle of the programme.

"Long-range precision strike is fundamental to credible deterrence," said Dr James Hartley, Defence and Security Analyst at the Royal United Services Institute (RUSI). "The investment signals a strategic shift towards technological independence and reinforces NATO's eastern flank."

Cyber resilience and defence

The Strategic Defence Review places significant emphasis on cyber security and the protection of critical national infrastructure. The National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) has announced a £500m investment in Active Cyber Defence programmes.

Thales UK, which operates research and development facilities in Berkshire, has been awarded a lead contract to develop next-generation defensive cyber operations tools. The Government Cyber Action Plan, published on 5 January 2026, reinforces the UK's commitment to integrated public and private sector cyber defence.

Berkshire's strategic importance

Thales UK has announced investment in next-generation cybersecurity and advanced systems facilities in Berkshire. The Atomic Weapons Establishment has also launched a recruitment campaign for advanced materials scientists and cyber specialists.

Mark Thompson, Chief Executive of the CBI Defence and Security Industries Association, said: "The Strategic Defence Review provides the clarity the defence industrial base needs to invest in R&D, manufacturing capacity and workforce development."

Broader implications

The review reflects a recalibration of UK defence policy in response to evolving threats in Europe and the Indo-Pacific. The commitment to sustained investment in modernisation of platforms and development of autonomous systems and AI-enhanced capabilities represents a departure from post-Cold War defence posture.

Government officials stress that defence spending increases will be matched by investment in cyber defence and resilience. The NCSC's expanded role, coordinating both offensive and defensive cyber operations, underscores the recognition that technological superiority is essential to deterrence and national security.
